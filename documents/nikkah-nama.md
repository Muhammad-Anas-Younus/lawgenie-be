## What is a Nikah Nama?

The Nikah Nama is the official Islamic marriage contract registered under the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO) 1961. It is a legal document issued by the Union Council and signed by the husband, wife, witnesses, and the Nikah Registrar (Nikah Khwan). It contains approximately 25 columns documenting the terms and conditions of the marriage.

**Urdu:** نکاح نامہ ایک سرکاری دستاویز ہے جو مسلم فیملی لاز آرڈیننس 1961 کے تحت رجسٹر ہوتی ہے۔

Without a registered Nikah Nama, a marriage may be legally valid under Islamic law but cannot be enforced in Pakistani civil courts. Always ensure your Nikah is registered with the local Union Council.

## Column 1 — Serial Number (سلسلہ نمبر)

**What it is:** The unique registration number assigned by the Union Council.

**Why it matters:** This number is used to retrieve your Nikah record from the Union Council. You will need this for NADRA marriage certificate applications and court proceedings.

**Common Questions:**

- _"How do I get a copy of my Nikah Nama?"_ — Visit your local Union Council with your serial number and CNIC. You can also apply for a NADRA marriage certificate using this number.
- _"I don't know my serial number."_ — You can trace it by visiting the Union Council of the area where your Nikah was performed.

---

## Column 4 — Husband's Name and Details (شوہر کا نام اور تفصیلات)

**What it is:** Full name, CNIC number, age, and address of the husband.

**Why it matters:** Establishes the legal identity of the husband. If incorrect details are recorded, it can complicate divorce proceedings, maintenance claims, and custody cases.

**Common Questions:**

- _"What if my husband gave a fake name or CNIC on the Nikah Nama?"_ — This can be grounds to challenge the validity of the contract. You should consult a lawyer immediately and file a complaint with NADRA.
- _"My husband's name is spelled incorrectly. Is my Nikah still valid?"_ — Minor spelling errors do not invalidate the Nikah under Islamic law, but should be corrected at the Union Council for legal purposes.

---

## Column 5 — Wife's Name and Details (بیوی کا نام اور تفصیلات)

**What it is:** Full name, CNIC number, age, and address of the wife.

**Why it matters:** Same legal significance as Column 4 for the wife. Particularly important for establishing identity in maintenance, Khula, and custody proceedings.

**Common Questions:**

- _"I was a minor when I got married and my correct age is not recorded."_ — This may be relevant to a case under the Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929. Consult a lawyer — the marriage may be voidable.
- _"Can I update my address on the Nikah Nama after moving?"_ — Address on the original Nikah Nama cannot be changed. Your current address can be established through your CNIC and utility bills in legal proceedings.

---

## Column 8 — Wali (Guardian) of the Wife (ولی)

**What it is:** The name of the wife's legal guardian (father, brother, or other male relative) who consented to the marriage on her behalf.

**Why it matters:** Under Hanafi Islamic law (followed in Pakistan), a wali's consent is strongly preferred. However, Pakistani civil courts (under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act) have upheld the validity of marriages where an adult woman consented herself.

**Common Questions:**

- _"My father did not consent to my Nikah. Is it valid?"_ — Under Pakistani civil law, an adult woman's own consent is sufficient. The Nikah is generally considered valid. However, the absence of a wali may complicate matters in some religious rulings.
- _"My wali was not present at the Nikah. Is this a problem?"_ — The wali can also give consent in absentia through a written authorization. If no wali was involved at all, consult a lawyer about the specific circumstances.

## Column 13 — Mehr (Dower) Amount (مہر)

**What it is:** The mandatory financial gift that the husband is obligated to pay the wife as part of the Nikah contract. It is the wife's exclusive right and cannot be waived without her free consent.

**Mehr has two parts:**

- **Prompt Mehr (معجل مہر / Mu'ajjal):** Payable immediately at the time of Nikah or on demand.
- **Deferred Mehr (مؤجل مہر / Mu'ajjal):** Payable at a future date, upon divorce, or upon the husband's death.

**Why it matters:** Mehr is one of the most commonly disputed items in divorce and separation cases. It is a legally enforceable debt owed by the husband to the wife.

**Common Questions:**

- _"My husband never paid my Mehr. Can I claim it?"_ — Yes. Mehr is a legal right enforceable in Pakistani family courts. You can file a suit for recovery of Mehr in the Family Court under the West Pakistan Family Courts Act 1964.
- _"My husband says I waived my Mehr. Is that valid?"_ — A waiver of Mehr is only valid if made by the wife freely and voluntarily, without any pressure. Forced or coerced waiver is not legally binding.
- _"What happens to my Mehr if I file for Khula?"_ — In most Khula cases, the court may ask the wife to return the prompt Mehr as a condition of the Khula. Deferred Mehr is treated differently. Consult a lawyer for your specific case.
- _"My Mehr was set at a very low amount (e.g., PKR 32). Is that valid?"_ — Islamically, any agreed amount is valid. However, courts can consider whether the amount was adequate and may factor this into maintenance decisions.
- _"My Nikah Nama says my Mehr is gold/property instead of cash. Is that valid?"_ — Yes. Mehr can be cash, gold, property, or any agreed asset. The court will determine its value at the time of the claim.

## Column 17 — Conditions Stipulated by Wife (بیوی کی طرف سے شرائط)

**What it is:** Any conditions or terms that the wife wished to impose at the time of marriage, which the husband agreed to.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the most underused but powerful columns in the Nikah Nama. If filled correctly, the wife can protect her rights from the outset.

**Examples of conditions a wife can include:**

- The right to work or continue education
- The right to live separately from in-laws
- Prohibition on the husband taking a second wife without her consent
- Specific financial arrangements

**Common Questions:**

- _"My column 17 is blank. Does that mean I have no rights?"_ — No. A blank column 17 means no additional conditions were set, but your baseline legal and Islamic rights are still fully intact.
- _"I wanted conditions but the Nikah Khwan didn't fill this column. What can I do?"_ — Unfortunately, conditions not recorded at the time of Nikah cannot be added later. You can negotiate a separate written agreement (postnuptial agreement) with your husband, though enforcement is more complex.
- _"My condition was written in Column 17 but my husband is violating it. What can I do?"_ — A condition in Column 17 is legally binding. You can file a suit in Family Court for enforcement or use the violation as grounds for dissolution of marriage.

## Column 18 — Tafwiz-e-Talaq / Delegated Right of Divorce (تفویض طلاق)

**What it is:** This column records whether the husband has delegated his right of divorce (Talaq) to the wife. If this column says **"Yes" (ہاں)**, the wife has the right to divorce herself without going to court.

**This is the single most important column for women's rights in the Nikah Nama.**

**Why it matters:** Without delegation in Column 18, a wife cannot give herself Talaq. She must either get the husband to pronounce Talaq, or go to court to file for Khula (which is a longer process). With Column 18 filled, she has the same power to end the marriage as the husband.

**Common Questions:**

- _"My Column 18 is blank or says 'No'. What are my options for divorce?"_
  — You must either: (a) ask your husband to pronounce Talaq, (b) file for Khula in Family Court, or (c) file for Dissolution of Marriage under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939.
- _"My Column 18 says 'Yes'. How do I use this right?"_
  — You can pronounce divorce upon yourself (using the delegation). You must then send a written Talaq notice to the Chairman of your local Union Council AND to your husband. The Arbitration Council will then be formed. The divorce becomes effective after the Iddat period (approximately 90 days).
- _"My husband says Column 18 doesn't count. Is he right?"_
  — No. A delegation recorded in Column 18 is legally binding under Pakistani law and Islamic jurisprudence. Your husband cannot unilaterally revoke it after the Nikah.
- _"Can I get the right of divorce added to my Nikah after marriage?"_
  — Not through the Nikah Nama. However, your husband can give you Tafwiz-e-Talaq through a separate written declaration at any time during the marriage.

## Column 19 — Other Conditions (دیگر شرائط)

**What it is:** A catch-all column for any other agreed conditions not covered in Column 17 or 18.

**Why it matters:** Similar to Column 17, this can be used to record additional agreements between the husband and wife.

**Common Questions:**

- _"There are conditions in Column 19 that I never agreed to. What do I do?"_ — If you did not sign or consent to those conditions, you can challenge their validity in court. Your signature at the bottom of the Nikah Nama is key evidence.
- _"My Column 19 has a condition that my husband must provide a house. He hasn't. What can I do?"_ — Failure to fulfill a condition in Column 19 can be used as a ground for dissolution of marriage or a civil suit for enforcement.

---

## Column 20 — Polygamy Declaration (تعدد ازدواج)

**What it is:** Records whether the husband is already married, and if so, whether he has obtained permission from the existing wife/wives and the Arbitration Council as required under MFLO 1961.

**Why it matters:** Under Section 6 of the MFLO 1961, a husband cannot contract a second marriage without: (a) prior permission of the Arbitration Council, and (b) consent of the existing wife. Violation is a criminal offense and the existing wife can file for dissolution of marriage.

**Common Questions:**

- _"My husband married someone else without my permission. What can I do?"_ — This is a violation of MFLO 1961 Section 6. You can: (a) file a criminal complaint against your husband (punishable by imprisonment up to 1 year and/or fine), and (b) file for dissolution of marriage on this ground alone under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act.
- _"I am the second wife. Is my Nikah valid?"_ — Islamically, it may be valid. However, under Pakistani civil law, if proper permission was not obtained, the second marriage is an offense and certain civil rights may be affected. Consult a lawyer.
- _"Column 20 says my husband was not previously married but he was. Is this fraud?"_ — Yes. Misrepresentation in the Nikah Nama is fraud and can be used to challenge the marriage contract and file criminal charges.

## Column 21 — Witnesses (گواہان)

**What it is:** Names and signatures of two adult Muslim male witnesses (or one male and two female witnesses under some interpretations) who were present at the Nikah.

**Why it matters:** Witnesses are a mandatory requirement for a valid Islamic Nikah. Their presence and signatures validate the contract.

**Common Questions:**

- _"There is only one witness on my Nikah Nama. Is my Nikah valid?"_ — Under Hanafi law, two witnesses are required for a valid Nikah. You should consult both a lawyer and an Islamic scholar (Mufti) to assess the validity of your specific situation.
- _"I don't know who the witnesses are. Does that matter?"_ — For most legal purposes (divorce, maintenance, custody), the registered Nikah Nama itself is sufficient proof of marriage. Witness identity only becomes critical if the marriage's validity is directly challenged.

---

## Column 22 — Nikah Registrar (Nikah Khwan) Details (نکاح رجسٹرار)

**What it is:** Name, license number, and signature of the Union Council-authorized Nikah registrar who performed and recorded the Nikah.

**Why it matters:** Only licensed Nikah Khwans can legally register a Nikah. An unregistered Nikah (performed without a licensed Nikah Khwan) is not recognized by Pakistani civil courts, even if it is Islamically valid.

**Common Questions:**

- _"My Nikah was performed by a local imam, not a licensed Nikah Khwan. Is my marriage registered?"_ — Likely not. An unregistered Nikah is Islamically valid but not legally enforceable. You cannot access civil court protections (maintenance, custody, inheritance enforcement) without registration. Consult a lawyer about getting it registered retrospectively.
- _"How do I verify my Nikah Khwan's license?"_ — You can check with your local Union Council or the relevant District Administration office.

---

## Column 25 — Signature of Wife (بیوی کے دستخط یا انگوٹھے کا نشان)

**What it is:** The wife's signature or thumbprint confirming her free consent to the marriage and the terms of the Nikah Nama.

**Why it matters:** This is the most critical element confirming the wife's consent. A Nikah without the wife's free consent is void under both Islamic law and Pakistani civil law.

**Common Questions:**

- _"My signature was forged on the Nikah Nama. What can I do?"_ — Forgery of a legal document is a criminal offense under the Pakistan Penal Code. You can file an FIR and challenge the marriage's validity in Family Court.
- _"I was forced to sign. What are my rights?"_ — A Nikah contracted under duress or coercion is voidable. You can file for dissolution of marriage in Family Court citing absence of free consent.
- _"My thumbprint was used instead of a signature. Is that valid?"_ — Yes, a thumbprint is legally valid as a form of consent, provided it was given freely.

## General Nikah Nama Q&A

**Q: My husband has the only copy of the Nikah Nama. How do I get my copy?**
A: You are legally entitled to a copy. Visit the Union Council where your Nikah was registered with your CNIC and the approximate date of your Nikah. You can also apply for an official NADRA Marriage Certificate which serves the same purpose.

**Q: Can a Nikah Nama be challenged in court?**
A: Yes. Grounds for challenging include: fraud or misrepresentation, absence of free consent, forgery, the wife being a minor, or non-compliance with MFLO 1961 requirements.

**Q: My Nikah was performed abroad. Is it valid in Pakistan?**
A: Generally yes, if it was valid under the law of the country where it was performed. However, you should register it with the Pakistani Embassy or, upon return, with the relevant Union Council to access civil law protections.

**Q: I had a court marriage (civil marriage). Do I have a Nikah Nama?**
A: A court marriage in Pakistan still involves a Nikah performed by a licensed Nikah Khwan and registered with the Union Council. You should have a Nikah Nama and can obtain a NADRA Marriage Certificate.

**Q: What is the difference between a Nikah Nama and a NADRA Marriage Certificate?**
A: The Nikah Nama is the original contract signed at the time of marriage and held at the Union Council. The NADRA Marriage Certificate is an official government certificate issued by NADRA that serves as proof of marriage for legal, travel, and administrative purposes. Both are important.

**Q: My husband pronounced Talaq verbally. Is the divorce complete?**
A: Under MFLO 1961 Section 7, a verbal Talaq is not effective until the husband sends a written notice to the Chairman of the Union Council. Only after this notice is sent does the 90-day reconciliation (Iddat) period begin. Without the written notice, the divorce is not legally recognized in Pakistani courts.
